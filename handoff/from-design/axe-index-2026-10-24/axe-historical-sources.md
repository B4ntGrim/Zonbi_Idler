# Zed Idler — Archaeological Axe Reference Register

Status: RESEARCHED REFERENCE / GAME INTERPRETATIONS REMAIN PROPOSED. Prepared 2026-10-24.

This register supports 30 historical anchor families and 300 fictional game designs. It does not claim 300 distinct excavated axe types. All survivor names, invented makers, variant silhouettes and stat affinities are fictional. A modern country is a location label, not the nationality of an ancient individual. Dates may describe an object, site or cultural period as explicitly noted. Ritual deposits, manufacture, burial, practical use and combat are distinguished.

The catalogue links each item to one H-number. The source supports the archaeological anchor, not its invented combat ability. Limited provenience and tentative interpretations are preserved rather than silently upgraded to certainty. Ten variants per anchor are game design variations, not ten archaeological specimens.

## Historical and art integration instructions

1. Use historical metadata in the Codex; label gameplay reconstructions as fictional adaptations.
2. Preserve each source's base form. Do not turn an adze or a mould into a proven combat axe; distinguish indirect evidence.
3. Do not imply that ritual thin-metal or jade prestige objects were heavy battlefield choppers. Combat-capable versions are survivor reforgings.
4. A cultural origin never grants an inherent stat advantage. Gameplay budgets come from construction, rarity, role and balance.
5. Exact measurements, grips, haft lengths and performance are not supplied by this index. Art and final models need a source review before claiming replica accuracy.
6. Add new regions through verified site/object records rather than assigning cultures based on appearance. This first set is weighted toward European excavated axes and does not claim global completeness.
